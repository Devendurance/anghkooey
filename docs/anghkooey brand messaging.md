# anghkooey brand messaging

## Document purpose

This is the build-ready messaging system for **Anghkooey**, a personal AI concierge that remembers how people like things.

The document covers:

- strategic message hierarchy;
- user insight and emotional framing;
- landing-page copy;
- product and conversational copy;
- memory, privacy, and trust language;
- cross-channel messaging;
- feature-to-benefit translation;
- hotel, travel, and dining use cases;
- Walrus Session 8 narrative;
- claims discipline;
- demo and pitch copy;
- objection handling;
- voice rules;
- copy testing and implementation priorities.

The name **Anghkooey** is locked. This document does not reopen naming.

# 1. The message to protect

## Primary promise

> **You shouldn’t have to explain yourself twice.**

This is the central line because it names the user’s frustration, not the technology. It is short, conversational, memorable, and broad enough to survive expansion beyond hotels, travel, and dining.

## Product descriptor

> **A personal AI concierge that remembers how you like things.**

Use this directly below the primary promise whenever the category needs to be made clear.

## Supporting explanation

> Anghkooey remembers the preferences, constraints, and past experiences that shape your choices—then brings the relevant context back when you need help again.

## Emotional payoff

> **Wait, you remembered that?**

This is not the headline. It is the emotional moment the product should create and the line to use in demos, launch stories, and user-reaction framing.

## Product job

> When I’m making a new decision, remember the small but important things I’ve already told you, so your help actually feels personal.

## Strategic enemy

> **Starting from zero.**

Anghkooey stands against:

- repeating the same preference to every assistant;
- generic recommendations that ignore lived experience;
- static profiles that become wrong;
- memory that cannot be inspected or corrected;
- assistants that imply they saved something when they did not;
- recommendations based on facts the system cannot verify.

# 2. Executive messaging diagnosis

## The product is not primarily about storage

“Persistent memory” is an implementation capability. It is not the user’s reason to care.

The user cares that:

- they do not have to retell the story about the noisy room;
- the next recommendation accounts for what went wrong before;
- an assistant remembers the difference between a firm constraint and a passing preference;
- an old preference can be changed;
- the assistant can show why its answer was shaped a certain way;
- their context does not become a permanent, invisible identity.

## The product is not primarily about travel

Hotels, travel, and dining are the first visible use cases. The larger territory is **personal context that compounds across decisions**.

Lead with the human problem. Use travel and dining as vivid proof because they make the value easy to see.

## The product is not primarily about AI novelty

Avoid making “AI” the reason to choose Anghkooey. The useful distinction is:

> **Other assistants answer the question in front of them. Anghkooey can bring forward the context you already gave it—when that context is relevant and actually saved.**

## The decisive strategic choice

Lead with:

> **Less re-explaining. More personal help.**

Support with:

> **Remember the preference. Preserve the reason. Update it when life changes.**

# 5. Core message territories

## Territory A — Stop starting over

**Role:** strongest acquisition territory.

> You already told the assistant what matters. Why start over next time?

Supporting lines:

- Less re-explaining. More useful help.
- Your context should not disappear when the chat ends.
- The next conversation can begin where the last one left off.
- Tell it once. Use it when it matters.

Best for:

- homepage hero;
- launch video;
- social posts;
- first demo scene.

## Territory B — Remember the reason

**Role:** strongest differentiation territory.

> A preference is more useful when the reason behind it is still there.

Supporting lines:

- Quiet matters because sleep matters.
- A constraint without context can be misunderstood.
- Anghkooey remembers the detail that changes the recommendation.
- The difference between “likes quiet” and “needs quiet after a terrible road-noise experience” is the difference between a label and useful context.

Best for:

- Walrus article;
- product explanation;
- technical demo;
- investor or judge pitch.

## Territory C — Change your mind safely

**Role:** strongest trust territory.

> What you liked before does not have to define you forever.

Supporting lines:

- Update the preference when life changes.
- The latest clear preference should guide the next answer.
- You are not locked into your old profile.
- Memory should be editable, not permanent by default.

Best for:

- privacy page;
- onboarding;
- memories page;
- correction flow.

## Territory D — A concierge, not a command centre

**Role:** strongest experience territory.

> Anghkooey helps you decide. It does not take the decision away from you.

Supporting lines:

- Compare the options you provide.
- Explain what fits and why.
- Be clear about what cannot be verified.
- Keep the user in control.

Best for:

- product UI;
- trust messaging;
- feature pages;
- roadmap boundary.

## 6.10 FAQ copy

### What does Anghkooey remember?

> Relevant preferences, constraints, and experiences you choose to share. When you give a reason, that reason can help Anghkooey apply the preference more carefully. Anghkooey should not treat every sentence as a permanent memory.

### Does it remember everything?

> No. The product is designed around relevant memory, not indiscriminate storage. The interface should tell you what was saved and when a save was not confirmed.

### Can my preferences change?

> Yes. Preferences are allowed to change. You can correct or update a remembered detail, and the newer clear preference should guide future help once the update is confirmed.

### Is Anghkooey a booking service?

> Not in the initial MVP. Anghkooey helps you think through options using your personal context. Do not assume live availability, prices, reservations, or completed purchases unless the product explicitly verifies them.

### Does it know my identity on every channel?

> No. Cross-channel continuity requires explicit identity linking. A name or phone number alone is not treated as proof that two accounts belong to the same person.

### Can I see or remove memories?

> Anghkooey should provide a review and correction path. The exact meaning of removal depends on the implemented storage and indexing behaviour, so the product must explain what a removal action does rather than promise more than it can guarantee.

# 8. Conversational copy system

## 8.1 Response order

When memory shapes an answer, use this order:

1. **Current answer** — answer the user’s question.
2. **Relevant memory** — state the recalled context.
3. **Reason it matters** — connect the memory to this decision.
4. **Evidence boundary** — say what is not verified.
5. **Next useful question** — ask only if it helps.

## 8.2 Hotel comparison template

> **Recommendation:** [Option A/B] looks closer to what you want.
>
> You previously said [preference] because [reason]. This option appears to fit because [specific evidence from current material].
>
> I can’t verify [live or external fact] from the information here.
>
> Is [one relevant trade-off] acceptable to you?

## 8.3 Travel-planning template

> You previously said [travel preference] for [context]. For this trip, [option] appears to fit because [current evidence].
>
> I would not apply that preference blindly if [changed context]. Do you want to keep the same priority for this trip?

## 8.4 Dining template

> You previously said [current food preference]. From this menu, [option] looks closest because [menu evidence]. The menu does not make [uncertain attribute] clear, so I’d ask before ordering.

## 8.5 Recall receipt

> **Why I suggested this**  
> You previously told me: “[short source-derived memory].”
>
> It matters here because: [connection to current decision].

Do not quote sensitive or unnecessary source text in full. Use the minimum relevant explanation.

## 8.6 No relevant memory

> I don’t have a relevant saved preference for this decision yet. If you tell me what matters most, I can use it here and ask whether you want it remembered.

## 8.7 Relevant memory but conflicting current request

> You previously prioritised quiet over centrality. For this trip, you’ve said being near the venue matters more. I’ll treat that as the current priority for this request. Would you like me to update the longer-term preference too?

## 8.8 Memory uncertain or stale

> I found an older preference that may be relevant, but I’m not sure it still applies: [preference]. Has that changed?

## 8.9 No permission to save

> I can use that for this conversation. I will not treat it as a saved preference unless you ask me to remember it.

## 8.10 User asks “Do you remember?”

> I remember [specific memory] from [source or earlier chat, if the UI can show it]. I’m using it here because [relevance]. You can correct it if it has changed.

If not remembered:

> I don’t have that saved. I can use it in this conversation, and I can ask to remember it for next time if you want.

## 8.11 User asks “What do you know about me?”

> I only want to show you context that Anghkooey has actually saved or retrieved. Here are the current relevant memories I can surface: [list].
>
> You can ask me to correct, stop using, or begin the available removal process for any of them.

## 8.12 User says “Forget that”

> I won’t use that detail in this conversation. Do you also want to start the available removal process for future recall?

If removal is immediate and verified:

> **Removed from future recall.**

If removal is pending:

> **Removal requested.** I won’t claim it is fully deleted until the system confirms what has been removed.

## 8.13 User says “You got that wrong”

> Thanks for correcting me. What is the current version you want me to use?

After response:

> I’ll treat [new preference] as the current preference. I’m confirming the update now.

## 8.14 Account or channel mismatch

> I can’t safely assume this channel belongs to the same Anghkooey account. Nothing has been merged. Please use the explicit linking flow if you want continuity.

## 8.15 Empty state

> **Nothing remembered yet.**
>
> Start with one small detail: what makes a hotel, meal, or trip work better for you?

CTA: **Tell Anghkooey one thing**

## 8.16 Loading state

> Looking through the relevant memories…

Do not say “I remember everything.”

## 8.17 Retrieval failure

> I couldn’t retrieve a confirmed relevant memory right now. I can still help from what you tell me in this conversation.

## 8.18 Model failure

> I’m having trouble forming a reliable answer right now. I can try again, or you can give me the specific options you want compared.

## 8.19 Memory write failure

> I could not confirm that the preference was saved. I’ll use it for this conversation only unless the save succeeds.

## 8.20 Unsupported action

> I can help compare options and remember your preferences. I can’t complete that booking or purchase in this MVP.

# 9. Memory and trust language

## 9.1 Plain-language memory explanation

> Anghkooey can keep useful context you choose to share so a future conversation does not have to start from zero. It should show you what it remembered, use it only when it is relevant, and let you correct it when it changes.

## 9.2 What is remembered

> Memory may include a preference, a constraint, a past experience, or the reason a choice matters—when you choose to share it and the product confirms it was saved.

## 9.3 What is not promised

> Anghkooey does not promise to remember every sentence, infer sensitive facts, identify you across channels without linking, or know live information it cannot verify.

## 9.4 Privacy positioning

> Personalisation should not require mystery. Anghkooey is designed to make memory visible, correctable, and bounded by what the system actually knows.

## 9.5 Consent language

> **Want me to remember this for future recommendations?**

Avoid:

> We will permanently store this forever.

unless the implementation, policy, and deletion semantics truly support that claim.

## 9.6 Source language

> **Source:** You told Anghkooey this in an earlier conversation.

If source is not known:

> **Source:** The system cannot show a reliable user-provided source for this item. Treat it as unconfirmed until you review it.

The product should not present a model inference as a user statement.

## 9.7 Confidence language

Prefer:

- Confirmed from your message
- Relevant to this question
- Older preference—please confirm
- Not saved yet
- Could not verify

Avoid:

- 99% sure this is what you want
- We know you prefer
- Your permanent preference

## 9.8 Security language

> Anghkooey uses explicit account and channel linking for continuity. A name or phone number alone is not treated as proof that two identities belong to the same person.

## 9.9 Credential boundary

Never request, store, display, or reproduce:

- passwords;
- API keys;
- access tokens;
- private keys;
- delegate keys;
- connection strings;
- secret codes except through an approved masked input flow;
- real test credentials in screenshots or documentation.

Public documentation uses placeholders such as `[REDACTED]` and fake example IDs only.

# 11. Product status and claims discipline

## 11.1 Public claim levels

### Level A — safe product truth

These describe the intended value without claiming a verified live implementation:

- Anghkooey is designed as a personal AI concierge that remembers how you like things.
- The product starts with hotel stays, travel, and dining.
- The experience is built around less repetition and more relevant help.
- Users should be able to review and correct remembered context.

### Level B — use only after live verification

- Anghkooey remembered this across a fresh session.
- This memory was written to Walrus Mainnet.
- Telegram and iMessage are linked to the same account.
- This memory was removed from future recall.
- The system successfully isolated user A from user B.

### Level C — do not claim without external evidence

- live room availability;
- live price comparison;
- completed booking;
- automated purchase;
- verified hotel noise level;
- guaranteed dietary safety;
- perfect or complete memory;
- automatic identity recognition;
- permanent deletion if the system cannot guarantee it.

## 11.2 Status labels inside product and docs

Use:

- **Shipped** — exercised in the current build.
- **Target** — in the proposed MVP scope.
- **Roadmap** — not part of the current build.
- **Hypothesis** — value still to be tested.

## 11.3 Before/after language

### Generic assistant

> Here are popular hotels in Lagos.

### Anghkooey target behaviour

> You previously said quiet matters more than centrality because road noise disrupted your sleep. Of the descriptions you shared, this option seems closer. I can’t verify actual noise or live availability from the current information.

Label the second as target or verified according to actual test evidence.

## 11.4 Walrus wording

### Good

> Anghkooey uses Walrus Memory as the intended persistent memory layer for the MVP, so relevant user context can be stored and recalled across conversations when the implementation confirms it.

### Better after verified Mainnet evidence

> In this build, Anghkooey writes user memories to Walrus Memory on Mainnet and recalls them in later conversations. Here is the verified evidence: [link or ID].

### Avoid

> Walrus gives Anghkooey infinite memory and perfect recall.

# 12. Walrus Session 8 hackathon messaging

## 12.1 Short submission description

> Anghkooey is a personal AI concierge that remembers the preferences and experiences behind your choices. Tell it once that road noise ruined your sleep; return later with two hotel options; get help shaped by that memory instead of a generic list. Correct the preference when life changes. Built around Walrus Memory, Anghkooey turns persistent memory into a more useful, more accountable everyday assistant.

Adjust “built around” to the verified integration state before submission.

## 12.2 One-sentence judge pitch

> **Anghkooey remembers the reason behind your preferences and uses that memory to make the next hotel, travel, or dining decision more personal.**

## 12.3 Thirty-second pitch

> Most assistants can answer a question, but they make you repeat the context that would make the answer useful. Anghkooey is a personal AI concierge that remembers how you like things. Tell it that quiet matters more than centrality because road noise ruined your sleep. Later, start a fresh conversation with two hotel descriptions and it can bring that context back, explain why one fits better, and tell you what it cannot verify. When your preference changes, you can correct it. Walrus Memory is the intended persistence layer behind the experience.

## 12.4 Sixty-second pitch

> Anghkooey starts with a simple frustration: people keep explaining themselves to assistants that never seem to become personal. A returning traveler might say, “I stayed near a busy road and barely slept. Quiet matters more than being central.” A generic chatbot may forget that by the next conversation. Anghkooey proposes a useful memory, asks whether to keep it, and uses it later when the user compares hotel options. It remembers the preference and the reason, shows the memory receipt, avoids inventing live availability, and lets the user update the preference when circumstances change. The point is not to remember everything. The point is to remember what makes the next decision better.

## 12.5 Problem statement for article

> A chatbot that forgets context forces the user to do the memory work. Anghkooey moves that burden into a visible, correctable memory loop: tell it what matters, confirm what was heard, return later, see the context shape the answer, and update it when life changes.

# 14. Objection handling

## “Isn’t this just ChatGPT memory?”

> The value is not the word “memory.” Anghkooey is focused around a specific user loop: capture a meaningful preference and its reason, confirm it, apply it to a later decision, explain why it shaped the answer, and let the user correct it. The product should demonstrate that loop rather than claim memory as a novelty.

## “Why would I trust an assistant with personal preferences?”

> You should not have to trust invisible magic. Anghkooey should show what it remembered, ask before saving where appropriate, distinguish confirmed context from inference, and let you correct or stop using a memory. The product must earn trust through visible behaviour.

## “Will it remember everything?”

> No. Remembering everything is not the goal. Anghkooey is designed to keep useful context that improves future help, not to turn every sentence into a permanent profile.

## “What if my preference changes?”

> Tell it. The product is designed to treat preferences as current and editable. A newer clear preference should supersede an older one once the update is confirmed.

## “Does this book my hotel?”

> Not in the initial MVP. Anghkooey helps compare and think through options using your preferences. It should not claim live availability, prices, bookings, or purchases unless those capabilities are built and verified.

## “Does it know who I am on Telegram or iMessage?”

> Only after the relevant identities are explicitly linked. Anghkooey should not merge accounts based only on a name or phone number.

## “What if it remembers the wrong thing?”

> The product should show the proposed memory, provide a correction path, and avoid treating an uncertain or stale detail as permanent truth. Users can say what the current preference is.

## “Does Walrus store my whole conversation?”

> Describe only the implemented memory behaviour and the actual data policy. Do not claim that every conversation is or is not stored unless the system and policy are verified. Public copy should explain what Anghkooey saves, why, and how the user can control it.

## “Can I delete a memory?”

> Provide the exact supported action. If deletion is a request, say it is a request. If the system confirms removal from future recall but not every historical layer, say that. Do not use “deleted forever” as a shortcut for an unverified operation.

The master brand is Anghkooey. Keep feature naming descriptive and subordinate until a feature earns independent equity.

The overall capability and user concept.

### Memory receipt

The explanation showing which remembered detail shaped an answer.

The user-facing review area.

### Remembered

Confirmed save state.

### Saving

In-progress state.

### Couldn’t save yet

Failure state.

Explicit web, Telegram, or iMessage identity connection.

The most recent clear preference that should guide future help.

# 17. Copy standards for the product team

## Every memory-aware response should answer four questions

1. What does Anghkooey recommend or understand now?
2. Which remembered detail shaped that answer?
3. What current evidence supports the recommendation?
4. What is not verified or may have changed?

## Every save interaction should answer four questions

1. What did Anghkooey hear?
2. Is it asking permission or confirming a user request?
3. Was the save confirmed?
4. How can the user correct it?

## Every correction interaction should answer four questions

4. What happens if it was not confirmed?

# 18. Testing plan

## 18.1 Comprehension tests

Show a new user only the hero and ask:

1. What is Anghkooey?
2. What does it remember?
3. What would you use it for?
4. What would you expect it to do next?
5. What would you worry it might do?

Success signals:

- user identifies it as a personal assistant or concierge;
- user understands that preferences can persist;
- user names less repetition as the benefit;
- user does not assume it books hotels automatically;
- user understands that memory is controllable.

## 18.2 Memory-loop test

Give the user this first input:

> I slept badly near a busy road in Lagos. Quiet matters more to me than being in the middle of town.

Measure whether the user understands:

- what was proposed as memory;
- whether the reason was captured;
- whether they can decline it;
- how to correct it.

Then run a fresh session and ask:

> Which of these two hotel descriptions fits me better?

Measure:

- whether the user notices recall;
- whether the answer is more useful than a generic list;
- whether the receipt is understandable;
- whether the user trusts the limitation statement.

## 18.3 Correction test

Change the preference:

> I’m okay being farther from the centre now if the room is quiet.

Measure:

- whether the current preference wins;
- whether the old preference is treated as history rather than current truth;
- whether the user understands the save state;
- whether the user knows what happens if the update fails.

## 18.4 Message A/B tests

### Hero A

> You shouldn’t have to explain yourself twice.

### Hero B

> A personal AI concierge that remembers how you like things.

Hypothesis:

- A will create stronger recognition of the pain.
- B will create faster category comprehension.
- Use A as headline and B as descriptor unless evidence reverses the hierarchy.

### CTA A

> Tell Anghkooey one thing

### CTA B

> Start your personal memory

Prefer A. It is clearer, warmer, and less likely to imply that the user must configure a profile.

## 18.5 Trust tests

Ask:

- Would you let this product remember the detail?
- Do you believe the save was confirmed?
- Do you know how to change it?
- Do you think it knows more than you told it?
- Do you believe it when it says it cannot verify live availability?

## 18.6 Deal-breakers

Stop or revise the design if users:

- assume Anghkooey can book or purchase without being told;
- think every conversation is permanently stored;
- cannot tell what was saved;
- believe a model inference was their own statement;
- cannot correct an old preference;
- assume name or phone number automatically links channels;
- think “remembered” means live facts are verified;
- confuse the Memories page with a complete data export.
